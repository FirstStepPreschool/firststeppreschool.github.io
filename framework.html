Our approach

The First Step Growth Framework

Six areas of development that shape every activity we plan for children aged 2 to 6.

Children between 2 and 6 learn through play, movement, repetition and conversation — not through worksheets alone. Our teaching is play-based and activity-based, using stories, music, art, outdoor play and our projector, TV and speaker setup to make each theme come alive.

01

Language & Communication

Listening, speaking, expressing

Between 2 and 6, a child moves from single words to full conversations. This is the base every other subject stands on.

At school

Circle time, rhymes, storytelling, picture talk, show-and-tell, phonics and daily conversation with teachers.

At home

Talk through your day with your child and let them finish their sentences without correction.

02

Early Numeracy & Thinking

Counting, patterns, reasoning

Numbers become real when children touch, sort and count actual things — not when they memorise them.

At school

Counting objects, sorting by shape and colour, patterns, comparison, puzzles and simple problem solving.

At home

Count stairs, sort vegetables, share biscuits equally — maths is already in your kitchen.

03

Body & Movement

Gross motor, fine motor, balance

Strong shoulders and steady fingers come before neat handwriting. Physical play is academic preparation.

At school

Daily play on our 3000 sq ft playground, action songs, dance, threading, tearing, pasting and colouring.

At home

Let your child climb, run and help with small tasks instead of doing everything for them.

04

Social Skills & Friendship

Sharing, cooperation, belonging

School is often a child's first community. Learning to share and take turns is a lifelong skill learnt here.

At school

Group activities, partner games, class responsibilities, festivals and our yearly gathering.

At home

Arrange simple playtime with other children and let them settle small disagreements themselves.

05

Feelings & Confidence

Emotions, self-regulation, courage

A child who can name what they feel is far calmer than a child who cannot. Confidence grows from being heard.

At school

Warm and patient teachers, predictable routines, stage performances and encouragement over correction.

At home

Name the feeling before solving the problem: 'You're upset because the tower fell.'

06

Creativity, Values & Independence

Imagination, character, self-help

Imagination and good habits are formed early. So is the pride of doing something without help.

At school

Art and craft, music, dramatic play, festival celebrations, values talks and self-help routines.

At home

Let your child pack their own bag and choose their own clothes, even if it takes longer.
