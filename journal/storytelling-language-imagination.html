How Storytelling Builds Language and Imagination

14 Jul 2026 · 5 min read

Before books, before worksheets, before phonics charts - there were stories. And in early childhood, stories are still one of the most powerful teaching tools we have. They cost nothing, they can happen anywhere, and they teach language better than most flashcards.

What stories give a child

  • Vocabulary: stories carry richer words than everyday conversation
  • Listening: a child follows a beginning, a middle and an end
  • Sequence: understanding “first, then, after” builds thinking
  • Imagination: a child pictures the jungle, the sea and the castle
  • Feeling: children feel brave with heroes and safe with kind endings

At school and at home

At First Step, storytelling is woven through every day - picture talk, puppets, action rhymes and stories told with our projector and speakers. At home, you do not need a single book to begin. Tell the story of the day you were born, or invent one about your child’s favourite toy.

Make it interactive

  1. Ask “what do you think happens next?” and wait for the answer
  2. Let your child change the ending - the sillier, the better
  3. Use voices, faces and sounds; children remember emotion
  4. Retell the same story often; repetition is how young children master language
The stories a child hears in their first years become the furniture of their mind.
