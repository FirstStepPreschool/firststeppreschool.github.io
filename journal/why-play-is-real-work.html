Why Play Is the Real Work of Childhood

12 Aug 2026 · 6 min read

If you have ever watched a child lost in play and wondered, “Is anything actually being learned here?” - the honest answer is: more than you think.

In early childhood, play is not a break from learning. It is learning. A child stacking blocks is experimenting with balance and cause-and-effect. A child playing shop is practising numbers, words and taking turns. A child pretending to be a doctor is building empathy and imagination.

What play really trains

  • Language: children talk, negotiate and explain while they play
  • Thinking: sorting, counting, comparing and solving problems
  • Movement: running, balancing, building and using small muscles
  • Feelings: practising fear, joy, frustration and patience safely
  • People skills: sharing, waiting for a turn and cooperating

Free play and guided play

Children need both kinds. Free play lets them choose, lead and imagine on their own terms. Guided play - where an adult adds a gentle idea or a new material - helps them stretch a little further. A good preschool blends the two: long stretches of free play, with caring teachers adding rhymes, stories and activities in between.

A child who plays well today becomes a child who learns well tomorrow.

What parents can do at home

  1. Make time for play every day - not only as a reward after “real” work
  2. Give open-ended materials: blocks, boxes, water, dough, paper
  3. Play with your child sometimes, and step back other times
  4. Talk about the play: “What are you building?”
  5. Let boredom happen - it is where imagination starts

At First Step, play sits at the centre of every classroom plan. When you see your child covered in paint or busy with sand, do not apologise for the mess. You are looking at the most natural learning machine in the world.
