Screen Time and Young Children: Finding a Healthy Balance

21 Jul 2026 · 5 min read

Most families today ask the same question: how much screen time is okay for a young child? There is no single perfect number, but there is a simple guiding idea - it is not just how long a child watches, but what they watch and what happens around it.

Why balance matters

Young children learn through their bodies, voices, hands and real relationships. Screens are useful, but they cannot hug, play, climb or talk back. When a screen replaces real play and real conversation for long stretches, the learning that matters most slows down.

Simple, practical rules

  • Choose slow, simple, age-appropriate content and watch it with your child
  • Talk about what you watched together - this turns passive viewing into language
  • Keep mealtimes and bedtime screen-free
  • Keep screens out of reach at night; young children need calm and darkness to sleep well
  • Make the real world slightly more interesting: keep books, blocks and outdoor time ready

The goal is not perfection

A tired parent on a rainy evening will reach for a video, and that is okay. The goal is balance, not perfection. What children need most is not zero screens - it is regular doses of real world: running outside, making a mess with art, telling stories, and being listened to.
